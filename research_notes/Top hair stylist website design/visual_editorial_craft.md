# Visual and editorial design craft of top-end hair salon and boutique beauty studio websites

Method and access notes (read first). Findings marked "source inspection" come from fetching each site's live home page HTML and its first-party stylesheets on 2026-11-14 and counting what is in the code: declared font families and weights, font sizes, colour values, `@keyframes`, image and video tags, visible words, header links. I did not render the pages or take screenshots, so anything about how a page looks on screen (which colour fills which area, how much of a phone screen an image covers) is inferred from code and is labelled as such. Word counts include footer and duplicated mobile-menu text unless stated. Sites that would not load are listed in the Gaps of the relevant section. "Award-level" hair salon sites are rare: I found only three salon entries with a confirmed award (two Awwwards, one CSS Nectar), so most examples are well-regarded live sites rather than award winners.

## 1. Typographic choices (display serif versus sans, sizes, number of weights) and how much copy appears per screen

### Takeaway
The edited sites use one or two type families and two to four weights in total, with display sizes that are large but not enormous (roughly 45 to 65px maximum on the calmest sites) and tiny, letter-spaced uppercase labels for everything secondary. Home pages of the calmest salon sites carry about 250 to 800 visible words in total, and most blocks hold a heading of 2 to 13 words plus zero to one short paragraph.

### Cited Findings

Type families and weights (source inspection, 2026-11-14)

- Spoke & Weal (10 US salons, custom Next.js build) declares exactly two families: Instrument Serif (400 and 400 italic) for display and Inter (300, 400, 500, 600) for text. Display sizes are fluid and modest: `clamp(2.1rem, 4.34vw, 3.92rem)`, `clamp(2.4rem, 4.6vw, 3.8rem)`, `clamp(1.7rem, 2.9vw, 2.6rem)`, so the largest heading tops out near 63px. Labels are 0.68 to 0.72rem uppercase with 0.22 to 0.3em letter-spacing. A class named `italic-serif` is used 27 times, so emphasis inside headings is done by switching to serif italic, not by weight or colour. Text columns are capped by character count (`max-w-[36ch]` 11 times, `max-w-[18ch]` 10 times, `max-w-[52ch]`, `max-w-[54ch]`). — [spokeandweal.com](https://spokeandweal.com/)
- George Northwood (London) declares two families from one superfamily: Suisse Int'l (400, 500) and Suisse Works serif (400). Three weights in total. Largest declared font size is 2.8125rem (45px). — [georgenorthwood.com](https://www.georgenorthwood.com/)
- Blue Tit London (about 20 salons) uses a single family, "Ekstra", in two weights only (400 and 700). — [bluetitlondon.com](https://bluetitlondon.com/)
- Buller + Rice (London, three salons) uses a single serif, Newsreader, at one weight (400). — [bullerandrice.com](https://www.bullerandrice.com/)
- Larry King (London) uses one family, GT America Condensed, in four styles (Medium, Ultra Light, Thin, Medium Italic). — [larrykinghair.com](https://larrykinghair.com/)
- Bleach London uses Termina, a wide geometric sans, for almost everything (340 `font-family` uses, weights 300 to 800), with Gotham (33 uses) and Antique Olive (27 uses) as minor voices. Largest declared size is 5rem. — [bleachlondon.com](https://bleachlondon.com/)
- Fleur Noire Tattoo (New York and San Diego, Squarespace) uses one family, Poppins. Bang Bang NYC (tattoo, Squarespace) uses only monospace: Space Mono plus Droid Sans Mono. — [fleurnoiretattoo.com](https://www.fleurnoiretattoo.com/); [bangbangforever.com](https://www.bangbangforever.com/)
- Nice Tattoo Parlor (Brooklyn, Squarespace) pairs Chivo (400, 700, 900) with Quicksand, and sets type by viewport width: declared sizes include 10vw, 9.375vw, 7.2vw and 6.25vw. — [nicetattooparlor.com](https://www.nicetattooparlor.com/)
- Shoreditch Nails (London) uses Inter Tight (500, 600, 700) for headings and Inter (400, 700) for text. — [shoreditchnails.com](https://www.shoreditchnails.com/)
- Townhouse (UK nail salons) sets headings in Ivy Presto Display at weight 300 (a light high-contrast serif) with Mallory Light as body, and its CSS contains very large viewport-based sizes (35vw, 22vw, 18vw). — [townhouse.co.uk](https://townhouse.co.uk/)
- Paintbox (New York nail studio) uses Freight Display Pro (serif) with Proxima Nova (sans); uppercase labels carry 2.2px letter-spacing (22 declarations). — [paint-box.com](https://paint-box.com/)
- Josh Wood Colour uses Brown in three weights (Light, Regular, Bold) plus PP Editorial New Light and Adobe Garamond as serif accents. — [joshwoodcolour.com](https://joshwoodcolour.com/)
- Salon SCK (New York), repeatedly cited in roundups as "elegant editorial", uses EB Garamond with Montserrat. — [salonsck.com](https://www.salonsck.com/); roundup description at [Colorlib](https://colorlib.com/wp/hair-salon-websites-design/)
- Effectownia (Warsaw; CSS Nectar Site of the Day, 12 May 2025) uses Catamaran for headings and Open Sans for text. — [effectownia.pl](https://effectownia.pl/); award listing at [CSS Nectar](https://cssnectar.com/css-gallery-inspiration/effectownia-fryzjer/)
- Bold colour brands outside salons follow the same two-family rule: Starface uses ABC Diatype Rounded (Heavy and Black) for headings and GT Pressura Mono for body; Chillhouse uses Suisse Int'l Condensed, Suisse Works and Sofia Pro; Good Dye Young uses Integral CF for display with Acumin Pro; Pulp Riot uses Avenir Next Condensed Demi with Source Code Pro. — [starface.world](https://starface.world/); [chillhouse.com](https://chillhouse.com/); [gooddyeyoung.com](https://gooddyeyoung.com/); [pulpriothair.com](https://www.pulpriothair.com/)
- Fonts In Use confirms the Starface system: "ABC Diatype Rounded" (Dinamo) and "GT Pressura Mono" (Grilli Type), identity by Someone & Others, logo based on Sniglet with custom star-shaped counters. — [Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/62820/starface)
- Typewolf's most recent Site of the Day entries (as served on 2026-11-14, latest entry dated Dec 14, 2025) almost all pair one characterful serif or display face with one neutral grotesque: Grenette + Styrene; DaVinci + Suisse Int'l; Editorial Old + Neue Montreal; Signifier + Switzer; Arizona Flare + Dia; Kabel + Neue Haas Grotesk; Tobias + Diatype. Typewolf's all-time "most popular" list is led by Apercu, GT America, Futura, Founders Grotesk, Neue Haas Grotesk, Canela, Graphik. — [Typewolf Site of the Day](https://www.typewolf.com/site-of-the-day)
- Counter-example of typographic clutter: Fox & Jane (multi-city salon group, WordPress with Elementor) declares about 25 font families in its stylesheets (Comfortaa, nine Futura cuts, Radikal, Lato, Pacifico, Marker Felt, Phosphate Inline, Agrandir Grand and Wide) and font sizes up to 200px. — [foxandjanesalon.com](https://foxandjanesalon.com/)

Copy length (source inspection, 2026-11-14)

- Spoke & Weal home page: 754 visible words in total. Per block: hero 25 words (an H1 of five words, "A full service hair salon.", plus a five-item service list and two links); mission 79 words (a 13-word H2, one 47-word paragraph, three statistics); ten "commitments" at about 10 words each (112 words); founder block 12 words; services block 246 words across five cards; press 54; Instagram 20; booking 62. — [spokeandweal.com](https://spokeandweal.com/)
- Suite Caroline (SoHo, New York) home page: 246 visible words including footer. Above the footer the page says only "Beauty Made With Intention" and "Book Your Look" (seven words), three product names, and five captions on portfolio images. — [suitecaroline.com](https://www.suitecaroline.com/)
- Blue Tit London home page: 266 visible words. Only one of its five tiles has a sentence (26 words); the others are a one- to four-word label on an image ("Workshops", "Gift cards", "Collections"). — [bluetitlondon.com](https://bluetitlondon.com/)
- Fleur Noire Tattoo home page: four content blocks of 16, 76, 25 and 10 words. The H1 is one sentence; one block is a single press quote from Time Out; the last is a one-line quote ("Fleur Noire is absolutely fucking gorgeous.") and a Book button. — [fleurnoiretattoo.com](https://www.fleurnoiretattoo.com/)
- Shoreditch Nails home page: hero is an image plus a two-word H1 ("CONSCIOUSLY COLOURFUL"); the next block is three images with two- to three-word labels (7 words); later blocks hold 35, 39 and 18 words. — [shoreditchnails.com](https://www.shoreditchnails.com/)
- Buller + Rice home page: 18-word positioning statement as the first H2, then one 167-word block covering three salons (about 30 words of description each plus address). — [bullerandrice.com](https://www.bullerandrice.com/)
- Hairrari (New York and Los Angeles) uses headings of two or three words only: "HAIR SINCE 2011", "LOCATIONS", "PRESS", "HAIRCARE + APPAREL". — [hairrari.com](https://hairrari.com/)
- George Northwood is the text-heavy exception among the edited sites: a 33-word hero statement set as one all-caps H2, followed by about 660 words of long editorial paragraphs interleaved with six images. — [georgenorthwood.com](https://www.georgenorthwood.com/)
- Retail-led sites in the same sector run several times longer: Hershesons 2,358 words and 226 image tags; Chillhouse 2,647 words, 155 image tags and 16 videos; Josh Wood Colour 1,263 words and 80 image tags. — [hershesons.com](https://www.hershesons.com/); [chillhouse.com](https://chillhouse.com/); [joshwoodcolour.com](https://joshwoodcolour.com/)
- An Awwwards-winning developer who presents the jury rubric says the Design score rewards a "Typographic system, not just a nice typeface" with consistent scale and hierarchy across breakpoints. — [Hon Tran, "Awwwards judging criteria", 22 Jul 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)
- A Squarespace designer's trend list says oversized type is for short text only: "Mega fonts work best for short pieces of text." and "Font style mixing works best for short headlines, promotions, or highlighted text." — [Paige Brunton, "12 Outdated Web Design Trends to Avoid in 2025"](https://www.paigebrunton.com/blog/outdated-web-design-trends-2025)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: there are two dominant typographic recipes. (a) "Editorial calm": a light or regular display serif (Instrument Serif, Ivy Presto, Freight Display, Suisse Works, Newsreader) with a neutral sans in small sizes. (b) "Confident single voice": one distinctive sans used for everything (Ekstra, Termina, GT America Condensed, Space Mono). Loud brands lean to (b) because a heavy or wide sans can carry attitude without adding a second decorative element.
- Interpretation: restraint shows more in weight count and size range than in the serif-versus-sans choice. The calm sites declare two to six font files; the cluttered counter-example declares dozens.
- Interpretation: on the calm sites the largest type is about 45 to 65px. The very large sizes (10vw and above) appear on the brands that use type as the personality device (Nice Tattoo Parlor, Townhouse, Fox & Jane), and then only on one to three words.
- Interpretation: copy budgets that recur are a headline of 2 to 13 words, a supporting paragraph of roughly 25 to 50 words, and labels of one to four words. Character-capped line lengths (18 to 54ch at Spoke & Weal) are what make short copy look deliberate.

### Gaps
- I could not measure rendered pixel sizes at specific breakpoints or words visible per actual viewport; "per screen" figures are per code block, which approximates but does not equal one screen.
- Squarespace sites set most heading sizes through site settings injected at runtime, so the maximum heading size for Fleur Noire, Bang Bang, Hairrari, Shoreditch Nails and Buller + Rice could not be read from static CSS.
- Bleach London renders its home page with JavaScript; only its stylesheets were analysed, so its on-page copy length is unknown.
- No design publication was found stating typical typographic norms for salon sites specifically; the pattern above is drawn from my own sample of about 30 sites.

## 2. Whitespace and pacing: ideas per screen, image size relative to viewport, section separation

### Takeaway
The edited sites give each block one job (one statement, one image set, or one list) and separate blocks by alternating full-width background bands (dark and light) or by full-height media, not by rules, boxes or cards. Heroes are typically one full-viewport image or video with five to fifteen words.

### Cited Findings
- Spoke & Weal's home page is ten sections, each tagged with a theme class, alternating in this order: ink (hero), cream (city ticker), cream-2 (mission), cream (commitments), ink (founder), ink (full-width video), ink (services), cream (press), ink (Instagram, `min-h-[78vh]`), cream (booking). The whole palette is two darks (#0a0a0a, #1d1d1d), two creams (#faf7f2, #f2ede3) and one accent (#c2a176 "honey"). — [spokeandweal.com](https://spokeandweal.com/) (source inspection)
- Spoke & Weal's hero is exactly one viewport tall (`h-[100svh]`, minimum 640px) and holds a background video, a five-word H1 and two links. A second media block is set to `h-[62vh]` on mobile and `md:h-[80vh]` on desktop. Service images use a fixed 4:5 portrait ratio. — [spokeandweal.com](https://spokeandweal.com/) (source inspection)
- George Northwood's CSS relies on full-viewport blocks (`h-screen` 17 occurrences, `min-h-screen` 6, `height:86vh` 5) on a neutral base (white, black, greys and one warm off-white #f1eae7). — [georgenorthwood.com](https://www.georgenorthwood.com/) (source inspection)
- Blue Tit London's CSS uses `height:100vh` and `height:100dvh` blocks and a portrait `aspect-ratio: 1/1.5` for images; the home page is five image tiles, one idea each. — [bluetitlondon.com](https://bluetitlondon.com/) (source inspection)
- Suite Caroline's home page sequence is: one hero image; one line of text and one button; three products; five portfolio images; footer. That is four ideas on the whole page. — [suitecaroline.com](https://www.suitecaroline.com/) (source inspection)
- Fleur Noire's home page alternates image-with-one-statement blocks and text-only quote blocks: image + H1, image + paragraph, quote only, image + quote + button. — [fleurnoiretattoo.com](https://www.fleurnoiretattoo.com/) (source inspection)
- Effectownia opens each section with a short "eyebrow" line above the H2 (for example "Twój pomysł. Nasza uwaga." above the heading) and numbers its six services 01 to 06 with about 20 words each. Spoke & Weal uses the same devices: eyebrow labels ("Our Mission", "Services", "Press") and numbered lists 01 to 10 and 01 to 05. — [effectownia.pl](https://effectownia.pl/); [spokeandweal.com](https://spokeandweal.com/) (source inspection)
- Bundy (Vienna) uses two- to four-word all-caps headings per section ("UNSERE Looks", "WELCOME TO THE CLUB", "DIE NEUE DEFINITION VON LUXUS") across about 18 sections. An earlier version of this salon's site won Awwwards Site of the Day on 3 May 2016 with tags "Big Background Images", "Clean" and "Video". — [bundy.at](https://www.bundy.at/); [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/bundy-bundy)
- The Readymag design team, writing for Awwwards, describes pacing as alternation: "By alternating design elements with negative space, a designer creates movement" and "The flow of composition determines how the eye is led through a design". — [Awwwards / Readymag, 22 Nov 2022](https://www.awwwards.com/readymag-design-team-explains-criteria-for-outstanding-websites.html)
- A developer presenting the Awwwards rubric lists "Grid discipline that still feels intentional, not templated" under Design, and says "A single strong idea carried through every section beats three competing ideas." — [Hon Tran, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)
- A Squarespace designer lists "Squish (Tight Layouts)" as outdated and "Space to Breathe" and "Mega-Tall Sections" as current: "Adding space makes your website feel modern, clean, and professional." — [Paige Brunton](https://www.paigebrunton.com/blog/outdated-web-design-trends-2025)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: "one idea per screen" in practice means one of four block types, used one at a time: statement (headline plus at most one paragraph), media (full-bleed image or video with no text or a caption), list (numbered services or locations), and proof (one quote or a row of press names).
- Interpretation: alternating dark and light full-width bands does the separating work that borders and cards do on template sites. For a black-walled studio this suggests black as the default band, with occasional light or single-colour bands as punctuation, rather than colour inside every band.
- Interpretation: numbering (01, 02, 03) and eyebrow labels give lists an editorial rhythm without adding decoration, and they appear on both a custom build (Spoke & Weal) and a Divi build (Effectownia), so they are cheap to adopt.

### Gaps
- Actual vertical spacing values between sections on most sites sit in shared utility classes or platform settings that I could not resolve to pixel values without rendering.
- No source gave a numeric norm for whitespace. The "one idea per block" pattern is my reading of the page structures.

## 3. Photography when the work is the product: full-bleed, single image, curated sets, grids, carousels, and how many portfolio images appear on the home page

### Takeaway
The most edited salon sites show very few work images on the home page (about five or six, each named or captioned) and send the rest to a dedicated page or to Instagram. Large uncaptioned grids appear only on sites where the grid itself is the concept.

### Cited Findings
- Suite Caroline shows five portfolio images on the home page, each with a title that names the client and the colour result: "Raquel Goes Red", "Platinum For Sabrina", "Raquel's Signature Blonde", "Carrie Coon's Blonde & Pixie Cut", "Michelle Williams Pink". — [suitecaroline.com](https://www.suitecaroline.com/) (source inspection)
- Bundy's home page shows a six-slide "UNSERE Looks" carousel and a separate before/after block labelled "VORHER" and "NACHHER". — [bundy.at](https://www.bundy.at/) (source inspection)
- Spoke & Weal shows no gallery of finished hair on the home page. Imagery is two videos (hero and a full-width services film with "Pause" and "Watch full screen" controls), one founder portrait and five 4:5 service images, one per service. — [spokeandweal.com](https://spokeandweal.com/) (source inspection)
- Blue Tit London's home page has five image tiles and no portfolio grid; finished work lives under "Inspo" and "Collections" in the menu. — [bluetitlondon.com](https://bluetitlondon.com/) (source inspection)
- Fleur Noire Tattoo shows four images on the whole home page and no portfolio grid; work is reached through per-city artist pages. — [fleurnoiretattoo.com](https://www.fleurnoiretattoo.com/) (source inspection)
- Bang Bang NYC presents 29 artists on one page, each as one image, a "VIEW GALLERY" link and a bio of roughly 40 words, so the home page shows one representative image per artist and links out for depth. — [bangbangforever.com](https://www.bangbangforever.com/) (source inspection)
- Nice Tattoo Parlor is the deliberate opposite: after a one-line header ("walk-ins welcome (646)-883-6417 open TUE-SUN from 12pm") the home page is a grid of 60 square images, uniformly cropped 1:1, with no captions. — [nicetattooparlor.com](https://www.nicetattooparlor.com/) (source inspection)
- Fox & Jane follows a three-word H1 and a 40-word paragraph with a block of 20 images. — [foxandjanesalon.com](https://foxandjanesalon.com/) (source inspection)
- George Northwood's home page has 11 image tags in total, interleaved one or two at a time with long text. — [georgenorthwood.com](https://www.georgenorthwood.com/) (source inspection)
- Effectownia keeps portfolio ("Realizacje") as its own menu item and puts an Instagram feed section on the home page; Edwards & Co (Australia) also ends its home page with an Instagram feed. — [effectownia.pl](https://effectownia.pl/); [edwardsandco.com.au](https://www.edwardsandco.com.au/) (source inspection)
- Good Dye Young uses colour itself as navigation imagery: its menu has a "Shop By Color" row of eight swatch images (Red, Blue, Green, Purple, Pink, Orange, Yellow, Neutral). — [gooddyeyoung.com](https://gooddyeyoung.com/) (source inspection)
- Marco Ambrosi Salon (Italian colour and extensions specialist) received an Awwwards Honorable Mention on 2 Jun 2021; the listing says the site "shows the process of his creative work through video and animations" and tags it "Photo & Video". — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/marco-ambrosi-salon)
- A roundup describes Strange Bird (Austin) as using "High-contrast B&W hair photography" that "reads editorial-premium", and Salon SCK as "large-format photography, minimal navigation". These are third-party descriptions; I could not load Strange Bird, and its domain now redirects elsewhere. — [Colorlib roundup](https://colorlib.com/wp/hair-salon-websites-design/)
- A developer presenting the Awwwards rubric says of Content: "A gorgeous shell wrapped around thin, generic copy reads as a template demo", and that stock-grade imagery weakens the score. — [Hon Tran, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: the premium signal is curation plus naming. Five captioned results ("Michelle Williams Pink") read as a body of work; sixty uncaptioned thumbnails read as a feed. For a vivid colour studio, five or six hero transformations, each named by shade or client, is the closest match to the edited sites.
- Interpretation: a consistent crop ratio does as much as image count. Spoke & Weal fixes 4:5, Blue Tit fixes 1:1.5, Nice Tattoo fixes 1:1. Uniform crops make even a large set look ordered.
- Interpretation: when the work is very colourful, a rule that only the photographs carry saturated colour (see section 4) makes each image the focal point of its block.
- Interpretation: video is used in place of a gallery on the highest-budget site (Spoke & Weal), one clip per screen, with a pause control.

### Gaps
- I could not see the images, so I cannot report on art direction (lighting, backgrounds, colour grading, whether portraits are cropped to hair or full face). This needs a visual review of the sites listed.
- I found no published guidance on the ideal number of portfolio images for a salon home page. The "five or six" figure is observed on two sites (Suite Caroline, Bundy), not a documented norm.
- Atelier Eva (tattoo, Brooklyn) has an "Our Work" section on its home page, but I did not count its images. — [ateliereva.com](https://www.ateliereva.com/)

## 4. How bold or maximalist brands stay uncluttered: named colourful examples and their restraint devices

### Takeaway
Colourful brands that read as well designed keep the interface nearly monochrome and let colour arrive through one channel only: the product or work photography, plus at most one or two brand accent colours. Personality is carried by voice and by one distinctive typeface, not by more elements.

### Cited Findings
- Starface (acne patches; identity by Someone & Others): the theme stylesheet contains essentially three colours, black (#000), white and one yellow (#fdff00). Other saturated colours (#ff3cb9, #1dd000, #00c3ff) appear only inline on individual product blocks. The theme CSS has zero `@keyframes` and seven transitions. Copy is all lowercase and conversational ("omg hi :) i'm big yellow!"). — [starface.world](https://starface.world/) (source inspection)
- Fonts In Use records the Starface identity as grounded in "a bright yellow color palette, Y2K design elements" and a single mascot, Big Yellow, with two typefaces. — [Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/62820/starface)
- Good Dye Young (vivid hair dye, co-founded by Hayley Williams): stylesheets are overwhelmingly black and white (#000 232 occurrences, #fff 186); bright values (#ff00aa, #ff5b00, #ff2554) appear once each, inline. Section headings are two to four words in caps ("NEW FAVES", "MOST POPULAR", "VIVID HAIR ESSENTIALS", "PIGMENTS WITH A PROMISE"). — [gooddyeyoung.com](https://gooddyeyoung.com/) (source inspection)
- Pulp Riot (professional vivid hair colour): stylesheet colours are almost only #000 (19) and #fff (7); one condensed sans and one monospace; home page is 14 images and 375 words; the slogan is two short sentences, "YOU ARE THE ARTIST. PULP RIOT IS THE PAINT." — [pulpriothair.com](https://www.pulpriothair.com/) (source inspection)
- Bleach London (colour-led salon and product brand): the base is neutral (white #fff, near-black #1a1a1a, greys), with a small set of brights that recur at equal frequency in the CSS (lime #b5e850, peach #fda68f, purples #e182f0 and #bc3ad1), and one typeface, Termina, doing nearly all the work. — [bleachlondon.com](https://bleachlondon.com/) (source inspection, stylesheets only)
- Collina Strada (fashion label known for saturated, maximalist collections): theme CSS contains only #000 and #fff, the largest declared font size is 1.15rem, and the home page has 13 images. The chrome is deliberately tiny and neutral. — [collinastrada.com](https://collinastrada.com/) (source inspection)
- Blue Tit London: black and white plus two pastels (light blue #a7d5ff, 46 occurrences; peach #fec6ae, 36) and one typeface in two weights. Voice does the personality work: "It's not just about hair, though. We respect your individuality, your creativity, and your freedom to self-express." — [bluetitlondon.com](https://bluetitlondon.com/) (source inspection)
- Shoreditch Nails positions itself in two words, "CONSCIOUSLY COLOURFUL", over a single hero image, with one sans family and a home page of about 150 words. — [shoreditchnails.com](https://www.shoreditchnails.com/) (source inspection)
- Nice Tattoo Parlor carries its friendly personality in micro-copy ("hm", "walk-ins welcome <3", a lone "♥" as a heading) and one uniform image grid, with two typefaces and near-black on white (#272727, #fff) plus one red (#ff0d00). — [nicetattooparlor.com](https://www.nicetattooparlor.com/) (source inspection)
- Fleur Noire Tattoo expresses attitude with a single swear word in a pulled press quote ("Fleur Noire is absolutely fucking gorgeous.") inside an otherwise plain one-typeface, four-image page. — [fleurnoiretattoo.com](https://www.fleurnoiretattoo.com/) (source inspection)
- Single-accent palettes recur across the sample: Spoke & Weal neutral plus honey #c2a176; Effectownia white and black plus plum #601b5f; Townhouse black and white plus blush #f7d2c0; Marco Ambrosi's Awwwards listing shows black #000, grey #9C9C9C and one orange #DF6C4F. — [spokeandweal.com](https://spokeandweal.com/); [effectownia.pl](https://effectownia.pl/); [townhouse.co.uk](https://townhouse.co.uk/); [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/marco-ambrosi-salon)
- Chillhouse (New York nail studio and product brand) has a disciplined palette (ink #010b13, white, peach #ffb990, periwinkle #9faee5) but a crowded home page: 155 image tags, 16 videos, 2,647 words and 87 header links. Palette restraint alone did not make it calm. — [chillhouse.com](https://chillhouse.com/) (source inspection)
- Fox & Jane uses four pastels (blue #9ad3e0, yellow #fad962, pink #f9bcbf, green #98d9b1) together with about 25 declared font families and 84 `@keyframes`. — [foxandjanesalon.com](https://foxandjanesalon.com/) (source inspection)
- Paintbox's identity and original website were designed by Lotta Nieminen (strategy by Figure NY, build by NoFavorite); Creative Boom's write-up says "Nieminen kept things simple, effortless and therefore elegantly unique." The article is from 22 Oct 2014 and contains no direct quotes from the designer. — [Creative Boom](https://www.creativeboom.com/work/nailing-a-new-brand-identity-for-paintbox/)
- A developer presenting the Awwwards rubric says colour should be "used deliberately, with restraint", and that stacking effects is read by jurors "as a lack of control". — [Hon Tran, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)
- A Squarespace designer argues the opposite of beige minimalism is now current: "Moving away from overly soft designs and embracing color can make your site feel current and engaging", and lists "White Backgrounds" as outdated in favour of "Anything But White". — [Paige Brunton](https://www.paigebrunton.com/blog/outdated-web-design-trends-2025)

### Inferences
- Interpretation, restraint devices seen repeatedly: (1) monochrome chrome, colour only in imagery; (2) one accent colour for interactive elements; (3) one typeface with attitude; (4) personality in short copy rather than in graphics; (5) very large type limited to two or three words; (6) uniform image crops; (7) colour used as navigation (swatches) rather than decoration.
- Interpretation for a black, hot pink and yellow brand: black is the natural base band. The closest analogues are Starface (black plus one yellow) and Good Dye Young or Pulp Riot (black and white chrome, product colour only). The evidence points to choosing either pink or yellow as the single interface accent, using the other sparingly or not at all in the interface, and letting client hair photos supply every other colour.
- Interpretation: the Chillhouse and Fox & Jane cases suggest clutter comes from element count (images, videos, links, typefaces, animations) more than from colour count. A loud palette with few elements reads as edited; a tasteful palette with many elements reads as busy.
- Interpretation: "maximalism" on the best sites is concentrated in one place per screen (a photo, a slogan, a swatch row) and surrounded by plain structure.

### Gaps
- I found no design-publication case study (It's Nice That, Creative Boom, Brand New, Dezeen) for a hair salon or colour studio with a bold colour identity from 2022 to 2026. Searches restricted to those publications returned only non-salon rebrands. The only salon-adjacent editorial case study found is Paintbox (2014).
- I could not find who designed the Bleach London, Blue Tit, Good Dye Young or George Northwood identities or websites; searches returned nothing attributable.
- Not Another Salon (London, known for vivid colour) could not be assessed: its domain returned a 404 page. Sang Bleu (tattoo) returned an "Under Construction" page. Danger Jones, Stevie English Hair, Vanity Projects, Treehouse Social Club, Mousey Browns and Nine Zero One failed to load from this environment. — [notanothersalon.com](https://www.notanothersalon.com/); [sangbleu.com](https://www.sangbleu.com/)
- Colour counts come from CSS text, not rendered area; a colour that appears rarely in CSS can still fill a large surface.

## 5. Motion: how it is used, how much, and what counts as tasteful versus gimmicky

### Takeaway
The calmest sites use a handful of named animations (about five), mostly fades and slow transitions of 300 to 700ms, one ambient video with a pause control, and they honour reduced-motion settings. Published guidance from Nielsen Norman Group and from Awwwards-side commentators agrees that motion should be short, purposeful and never take over scrolling.

### Cited Findings
- Spoke & Weal defines five animations in total (`marquee`, `page-enter`, `galFade`, `flip-hint`, `spin-slow`). Transition durations are mostly 500ms (`duration-500` used 32 times), with 300ms (10 times) and one 700ms ease-out. It includes `prefers-reduced-motion` rules and "Pause background video" and "Pause services video" controls. — [spokeandweal.com](https://spokeandweal.com/) (source inspection)
- Other edited sites are similarly sparse: Starface's theme has 0 `@keyframes`; Bleach London's stylesheets have 1 (a loading spinner); Suite Caroline 3; George Northwood 5 (utility fades and spins); Larry King 3 (`marquee`, `flipUp`, `flipDown`). — [starface.world](https://starface.world/); [bleachlondon.com](https://bleachlondon.com/); [suitecaroline.com](https://www.suitecaroline.com/); [georgenorthwood.com](https://www.georgenorthwood.com/); [larrykinghair.com](https://larrykinghair.com/) (source inspection)
- Heavier sites carry animation libraries: Fox & Jane 84 `@keyframes` (bounce, bounceIn, fadeInDownBig and similar from a stock animation library); Good Dye Young 23; Paintbox 23; Blue Tit London 26 (most belong to an image lightbox). — [foxandjanesalon.com](https://foxandjanesalon.com/); [gooddyeyoung.com](https://gooddyeyoung.com/); [paint-box.com](https://paint-box.com/); [bluetitlondon.com](https://bluetitlondon.com/) (source inspection)
- A scrolling text ticker (marquee) appears on many sites in the sample: Spoke & Weal (city names), Larry King, Good Dye Young, Paintbox ("scrolling-text"), Nice Tattoo Parlor, Bundy and Josh Wood Colour. — [spokeandweal.com](https://spokeandweal.com/); [larrykinghair.com](https://larrykinghair.com/); [gooddyeyoung.com](https://gooddyeyoung.com/); [paint-box.com](https://paint-box.com/); [nicetattooparlor.com](https://www.nicetattooparlor.com/); [bundy.at](https://www.bundy.at/); [joshwoodcolour.com](https://joshwoodcolour.com/) (source inspection)
- Nielsen Norman Group defines scrolljacking as "a design pattern that changes the speed and, sometimes, the direction of scrolling on a web page" and reports: "The majority of our study participants were at least mildly disoriented by scrolljacking." Its guidance: use it only where it adds functional value, keep important text out of it ("we advise against including important content inside of a scrolljack section"), "if you are going to use scrolljacking, use it below the fold", and avoid it on mobile, where the benefits are "in most cases" not worth the cost. — [NN/g, "Scrolljacking 101", Sara Paul, 6 Aug 2023](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)
- A developer presenting the Awwwards rubric: "A hero that hitches at 40fps reads as broken, not premium."; "always gate motion behind the user's OS preference."; janky motion costs more Usability points than it earns in Creativity; generic tropes such as cursor blobs and default smooth-scroll setups get discounted. — [Hon Tran, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)
- The same author on 2026 winners: "The motion has a director, not just a library."; a cited site is "a masterclass in restraint" whose transitions "never call attention to themselves"; "Beauty at 60fps is the whole discipline." Example sites named: By-Kin, Uncommon Studio, Mat Voyce. — [Hon Tran, 27 Jun 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)
- A Squarespace designer recommends "Subtle Movement", meaning "small shifts that bring the site to life without disrupting usability", and scroll fades, while warning that sideways sliders can hurt usability for less technical users. — [Paige Brunton](https://www.paigebrunton.com/blog/outdated-web-design-trends-2025)
- The Readymag design team, on Awwwards, says of animation features: "take these features as a source of inspiration, not a necessary requirement". — [Awwwards / Readymag, 22 Nov 2022](https://www.awwwards.com/readymag-design-team-explains-criteria-for-outstanding-websites.html)
- Both awarded salon sites were recognised partly for moving image: Bundy's 2016 Site of the Day has "Video" as its highlighted interaction element and an Animations sub-score of 7 out of 10; Marco Ambrosi's 2021 Honorable Mention is tagged GSAP and Scrolling. — [Awwwards, Bundy](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/bundy-bundy); [Awwwards, Marco Ambrosi](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/marco-ambrosi-salon)

### Inferences
- Interpretation, tasteful: fades and slight rises on entry, 300 to 700ms; one slow ambient video per screen with a pause control; hover colour shifts to the accent; one ticker at most; a soft page-enter transition; everything disabled under reduced-motion.
- Interpretation, gimmicky: altered scroll speed or direction, custom cursors, bounce and elastic entrances from stock libraries, several animated elements competing in one view, motion that delays reading the booking link.
- Interpretation: a count of roughly five distinct animations is a practical ceiling drawn from the calmest sites in this sample.
- Interpretation: the marquee ticker is now so common across both edited and busy sites that it no longer signals craft by itself; one ticker used for a factual list (locations, press names) is the restrained form.

### Gaps
- Keyframe counts measure CSS only. JavaScript-driven animation (GSAP, scroll libraries) is not captured, so sites may move more than their CSS shows.
- I could not watch any motion, so easing quality, timing and choreography are unverified.
- NN/g's scrolljacking study is from 2023 and is not specific to salon sites.

## 6. Current (2024 to 2026) trends for boutique service websites, and which are already overused or template-like

### Takeaway
Current boutique conventions are: serif display plus small grotesque, off-white or coloured bands instead of pure white, tall sections, oversized short headlines, numbered lists with eyebrow labels, a marquee ticker, fade-on-scroll, and a persistent Book button. Several of these are already platform defaults, so they no longer differentiate; commentators single out stacked effects, cursor tricks, default smooth scroll and scrolljacking as tired.

### Cited Findings
- A Squarespace designer's list of what is current for small-business sites: bold colour over "Light, White & Airy"; "Mega-Tall Sections"; images cropped into arches or ovals; "Subtle Movement"; content "Running Off the Sides of the Page"; fading elements; texture; font style mixing; "Mega Fonts"; "Space to Breathe"; "Anything But White" backgrounds. The post is dated Dec 30 with no year shown; its title refers to 2025. — [Paige Brunton](https://www.paigebrunton.com/blog/outdated-web-design-trends-2025)
- A developer presenting the Awwwards rubric says award winners are not "decorated templates" and need "A point of view"; weaker patterns are effects "bolted on", animation for its own sake, and generic tropes such as cursor blobs and default smooth-scroll setups. — [Hon Tran, 27 Jun 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026); [Hon Tran, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)
- Typewolf's recent Site of the Day picks show the serif-display-plus-grotesque pairing as the prevailing typographic recipe among curated sites (most of the nine entries listed on the page). — [Typewolf](https://www.typewolf.com/site-of-the-day)
- The same free typefaces recur across unrelated sites in my sample, which is a template signal: Poppins on Fleur Noire, Hairrari and Hershesons; Montserrat on Salon SCK, Hershesons and Half Magic; Inter on Spoke & Weal, Shoreditch Nails and Townhouse; Tenor Sans plus Outfit on both Paintbox and Good Dye Young. Both stores identify their Shopify theme as "Impulse" in the page source (Paintbox version 7.4.0, Good Dye Young version 5.3.0) and share its animation names such as `rise-up` and `slideshowBars`, so a calm nail studio and a loud hair dye brand sit on the same template and differ only in type, colour, imagery and copy. — [fleurnoiretattoo.com](https://www.fleurnoiretattoo.com/); [hairrari.com](https://hairrari.com/); [salonsck.com](https://www.salonsck.com/); [paint-box.com](https://paint-box.com/); [gooddyeyoung.com](https://gooddyeyoung.com/) (source inspection)
- Most small studio sites in the sample are built on a hosted platform: Squarespace (Fleur Noire, Bang Bang, Nice Tattoo, Atelier Eva, Hairrari, Shoreditch Nails, Buller + Rice, Bundy), Shopify (Suite Caroline, George Northwood, Larry King, Paintbox, Good Dye Young, Townhouse, Glasshouse), WordPress (Blue Tit, Effectownia, Fox & Jane). Spoke & Weal is the one custom Next.js build. — [bundy.at](https://www.bundy.at/); [suitecaroline.com](https://www.suitecaroline.com/); [bluetitlondon.com](https://bluetitlondon.com/); [spokeandweal.com](https://spokeandweal.com/) (source inspection)
- Search-level evidence (snippets from vendor trend articles, not opened, lower reliability): 2026 trend guides describe a "reaction against AI-generated sameness", say the best scroll animations "clarify what the content is saying, they don't decorate it", and warn that bento grids lose legibility when used as a whole system. — [Line25](https://line25.com/articles/web-design-trends-2026/); [Lovable](https://lovable.dev/guides/website-design-trends-2026)
- Search-level evidence (snippets, lower reliability): custom cursors are described by one 2024 trend piece as "a gimmick that reminds one of the 90s internet era", and hero sliders are flagged as generic. — [Vistaprint](https://www.vistaprint.com/hub/web-design-trends-2024); [Speckyboy](https://speckyboy.com/?p=71599)

### Inferences
- Interpretation, still effective: black or off-white bands instead of white; one confident typeface; short oversized headline; numbered service list with prices; named portfolio pieces; persistent Book button; one ambient video.
- Interpretation, now template-like (seen on many platform sites in the sample, so they add no distinction): Poppins or Montserrat headings; serif italic word inside a sans headline; arch-shaped image crops; beige with a thin serif; fade-up on every block; a ticker on every page; an Instagram feed grid as the portfolio; "As seen in" logo rows.
- Interpretation: Spoke & Weal's Instrument Serif plus Inter is tasteful but is also a very common free pairing, so the typeface choice is not what makes that site feel premium. Its restraint in size, weight, palette and copy does.
- Interpretation: for a loud brand, the beige editorial look is both overused and off-brand. The underused route in this sector is black-based, one accent, one bold sans, which few salon sites in the sample attempt.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative, non-vendor source (Awwwards, Webflow, Squarespace, a design publication) naming overused trends specifically for boutique service or salon sites in 2024 to 2026. The "overused" list is largely my interpretation of repetition across the sample plus one Squarespace designer and one award-side developer.
- The general "web design trends 2026" articles found are vendor marketing of uneven quality; I used only their snippets and flag them as weak.
- Typewolf's listing as served showed its newest entry as Dec 14, 2025, so it may not reflect 2026.

## 7. Navigation and menus on mobile, and how many items

### Takeaway
The edited studio sites keep four to eight top-level items, hide them behind a single menu control, and keep one Book button permanently visible beside it. Research guidance supports showing links openly only when there are four or fewer, and otherwise hiding some while repeating key links in the page.

### Cited Findings
- Spoke & Weal's header exposes one link only, "Schedule Now / Book", plus an "Open menu" control, on all screen sizes; the hero repeats the key choices as in-page links (five services, "Schedule Online", "See the Menu"). — [spokeandweal.com](https://spokeandweal.com/) (source inspection)
- Top-level menu counts observed: Buller + Rice 4 (Services, Salons, FAQ, About); Shoreditch Nails 4 folders plus a Book button; Effectownia 5 plus a Book button; Fleur Noire 6 plus "Book an Appointment"; Bundy 6 plus "TERMIN"; Hairrari 6; Suite Caroline 7 (Book Service, Menu, Team, Press, About, FAQ, Sustainability); Fox & Jane 7; Nice Tattoo Parlor 8; Bang Bang NYC 8 one-page anchors; Blue Tit London 11 plus "Book appointment". — [bullerandrice.com](https://www.bullerandrice.com/); [shoreditchnails.com](https://www.shoreditchnails.com/); [effectownia.pl](https://effectownia.pl/); [fleurnoiretattoo.com](https://www.fleurnoiretattoo.com/); [bundy.at](https://www.bundy.at/); [hairrari.com](https://hairrari.com/); [suitecaroline.com](https://www.suitecaroline.com/); [foxandjanesalon.com](https://foxandjanesalon.com/); [nicetattooparlor.com](https://www.nicetattooparlor.com/); [bangbangforever.com](https://www.bangbangforever.com/); [bluetitlondon.com](https://bluetitlondon.com/) (source inspection)
- The Squarespace sites (Fleur Noire, Hairrari, Shoreditch Nails, Buller + Rice, Bundy) ship the standard "Open Menu / Close Menu" overlay with nested "Folder / Back" panels for sub-pages, and the Book button is duplicated inside and outside the overlay. — [shoreditchnails.com](https://www.shoreditchnails.com/); [fleurnoiretattoo.com](https://www.fleurnoiretattoo.com/) (source inspection)
- Multi-location brands group booking by location inside the menu instead of adding top-level items: George Northwood lists each salon with "Online Booking, Team, Services & Prices, Find Us" beneath it; Hairrari has a "Book Cuts + Color" folder listing each location. — [georgenorthwood.com](https://www.georgenorthwood.com/); [hairrari.com](https://hairrari.com/) (source inspection)
- Retail-led sites in the sector carry far larger menus: Larry King 46 header links, Chillhouse 87, Glasshouse Salon 72, Pulp Riot 35. — [larrykinghair.com](https://larrykinghair.com/); [chillhouse.com](https://chillhouse.com/); [glasshousesalon.co.uk](https://glasshousesalon.co.uk/); [pulpriothair.com](https://www.pulpriothair.com/) (source inspection)
- Nielsen Norman Group's testing found hidden navigation was used in 57% of cases on mobile versus 86% for combined visible-plus-hidden navigation, and reduced content discoverability by more than 20%. Its recommendation: "If your site has 4 or fewer top-level navigation links, display them as visible links"; with more than four, "the only reasonable solution is to hide some of these", and provide in-page links to important content. — [NN/g, "Hamburger Menus and Hidden Navigation Hurt UX Metrics", Pernice and Budiu, 26 Jun 2016](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/hamburger-menus/)
- A developer presenting the Awwwards rubric frames the navigation test as: "can a first-time visitor orient in under three seconds?" and says a site "has to hold up on a real phone, not just a resized desktop window." — [Hon Tran, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: the working pattern for a single-location studio is logo, one visible Book button and one menu control; four to six menu items (for example Work, Services and prices, About, Visit, plus Book); and the two or three most important destinations repeated as links inside the hero so that nothing essential depends on opening the menu.
- Interpretation: a menu with more than about eight items is a sign that retail or multi-location content is steering the home page; the calm sites push that depth into nested panels.
- Interpretation: a full-screen overlay menu doubles as a brand moment (large type on a solid colour), which suits a bold brand better than a small dropdown.

### Gaps
- I could not render mobile layouts, so I cannot confirm whether any site uses a bottom bar, a sticky Book button on scroll, or how the overlay is styled. The observations come from markup.
- NN/g's quantitative study is from 2016; I found no newer study of comparable rigour, and none specific to salon or booking sites.
- No source specified an ideal number of menu items for boutique service sites; the four-to-eight range is observed, not prescribed.

## 8. How designers and juries describe what makes a small studio site excellent

### Takeaway
Juries score design most heavily and describe excellence as control: one idea carried through, a typographic system, deliberate colour, smooth and optional motion, and real content. Awarded salon sites are few, and the ones found won on large clean imagery and video, with palettes of black, grey and one accent.

### Cited Findings
- Awwwards weights its jury criteria as "Design: 40% points", "Usability: 30% points", "Creativity: 20% points" and "Content: 10% points". "HMs are awarded to sites that are scored 6.5 or more from the jury." — [Awwwards evaluation system](https://www.awwwards.com/about-evaluation/)
- Bundy Bundy (Vienna hair salon, site by agency WILD) won Awwwards Site of the Day on 3 May 2016 with 7.94 overall: Design 8.2, Usability 7.79, Creativity 7.68, Content 7.83. Its tags are "Fashion", "Animation", "Big Background Images", "Clean", "Video", and its listed colours are one blue (#2779a7), one grey (#9C9C9C) and white. — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/bundy-bundy)
- Marco Ambrosi Salon (site by studio zerotredici) received an Awwwards Honorable Mention on 2 Jun 2021. Listed colours are black, grey and one orange (#DF6C4F). The shown community votes average about 7.7 for Design, 8.0 for Usability, 7.3 for Creativity and 8.1 for Content (averages calculated from the 15 votes displayed, not an official aggregate). The original domain no longer hosts the salon site; it now serves unrelated blog content. — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/marco-ambrosi-salon); [marcoambrosi.it](https://www.marcoambrosi.it/)
- The only hair salon Awwwards Site of the Day surfaced by search besides Bundy is The Lounge (Helsinki), dated 23 Sep 2011. This comes from a search result summary; I did not open the listing. — [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/the-lounge)
- Effectownia (Warsaw) was CSS Nectar Site of the Day on 12 May 2025. — [CSS Nectar](https://cssnectar.com/css-gallery-inspiration/effectownia-fryzjer/)
- A developer with three Awwwards Sites of the Day, describing the rubric: "A single strong idea carried through every section beats three competing ideas."; creativity is "originality with intent", an idea that "couldn't be swapped onto any other brand"; winners "take one strong concept and execute it further than anyone expects." — [Hon Tran, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)
- The same author lists common mistakes: stacked effects ("The jury reads that as a lack of control and it costs you here."), animation that harms usability, desktop-only thinking, placeholder content, missing focus states and reduced-motion handling, and slow first load. — [Hon Tran, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)
- The Readymag design team, on Awwwards: "Good typography should guide and inform your viewers, optimize readability and accessibility"; "We firmly believe that ideas conveyed in the project must be original and coherent". One of its designers praises a project for "carefully-orchestrated chaos on the first page". — [Awwwards / Readymag, 22 Nov 2022](https://www.awwwards.com/readymag-design-team-explains-criteria-for-outstanding-websites.html)
- Creative Boom on Paintbox's identity and site: the designer "kept things simple, effortless and therefore elegantly unique." — [Creative Boom, 22 Oct 2014](https://www.creativeboom.com/work/nailing-a-new-brand-identity-for-paintbox/)
- Salon roundups (vendor blogs, lower authority) praise the same qualities in plainer terms: Salon SCK for "large-format photography, minimal navigation"; Fringe for "Professional editorial shoots combined with high-end typography". — [Colorlib](https://colorlib.com/wp/hair-salon-websites-design/); [Zarla](https://www.zarla.com/inspiration/salon)

### Inferences
- Interpretation: the phrase "carefully-orchestrated chaos" is the useful model for a maximalist brand. Juries accept intensity when it is confined to a place and visibly controlled elsewhere.
- Interpretation: the "couldn't be swapped onto any other brand" test favours using the studio's real distinguishing facts (black walls, two signature colours, vivid results, its own voice) as the one idea, rather than adopting the beige editorial salon look.
- Interpretation: since design carries 40% and content only 10% in the Awwwards rubric, jury taste rewards visual control first; but the content comments show that thin or generic copy and stock imagery pull a site back toward "template demo".
- Interpretation: the award record suggests salon sites rarely compete at this level, so a small studio site that applies these principles would stand out within its category.

- Awwwards does not publish written jury comments on individual sites, only scores and tags, so there are no verbatim jury remarks about any salon site.
- The Hon Tran posts are one practitioner's account. A related-post blurb calls him a jury member; the main article says his sites have been scored by the jury. I could not independently verify jury membership.
- I could not load siteinspire, godly.website, land-book, httpster, minimal.gallery or brutalistwebsites category pages for salons within the time available, so curated-gallery picks for this sector are not covered.
- No It's Nice That, Dezeen, Brand New or Webflow or Squarespace showcase article about a hair salon website from 2022 to 2026 was found. Buller + Rice states on its own site that its salons were "Recognised by the Dezeen Awards", which concerns interiors, not the website. — [bullerandrice.com](https://www.bullerandrice.com/)
