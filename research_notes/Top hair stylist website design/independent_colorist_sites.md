# Independent, solo and small-studio hair colorist websites (vivid / fashion-color focus): how they organise their sites

Method note (applies to every finding below): pages were loaded on 2026-11-14 with a fetch tool that converts each page to text and summarises it. That means (a) visual design, colour, type and image quality were NOT observed, only structure and copy; (b) word counts are the tool's approximations; (c) some long pages were truncated and are flagged; (d) JavaScript-embedded widgets (booking embeds, forms) often did not render. Platform identification is from asset URLs (e.g. `static.wixstatic.com`, `images.squarespace-cdn.com`). 15 sites loaded; 4 did not (listed under Gaps in the first section).

## How do well-known vivid / creative color specialists and respected independent colorists structure their websites? (specific examples with URLs)

### Takeaway
The sites that loaded fall into three shapes: (1) a very short home page that acts as a signpost to 5 to 7 sub-pages (most common among the vivid specialists: HDH, Bold Hearts, Shelley Gregory, Pinup Jordan), (2) a medium home page of about 8 to 9 sections that sells the experience and pushes one CTA (Nikki the Hair Artist, CRIS HAIR, The Decora House, Sunday Hair Studio), and (3) a long everything-on-one-page site carrying policies, FAQ or a full price list (Taylor Monroe, Kristen James, Luna, Cherry Blossom). Every vivid specialist that loaded is multi-page, and all of them put vivid-specific education (session length, cost logic, fading) on a sub-page or blog post, never on the home page.

### Inferences
- Among the vivid-identified sites that loaded (Nikki, HDH, Shelley Gregory, Bold Hearts, BK, Pinup Jordan), the home page is short: roughly 80 to 650 words. The long home pages belong to non-vivid generalists or lead-gen style builds (Taylor Monroe, Cherry Blossom, Kristen James).
- Shelley Gregory's footer year (2016) and the "/new-page" slug on her About page suggest the site has not been restructured for years; it likely functions as a static portfolio while Instagram does the marketing. This is an inference from the footer and slug, not a stated fact.
- Rebecca Taylor's domain being a coaching site suggests that some well-known vivid artists no longer use their personal domain for client booking at all (education and coaching become the product). Based on one example only.
- "Well-known" status of Shelley Gregory and Rebecca Taylor as vivid artists is from general background knowledge, not from a source loaded in this session.

### Gaps
- Could not load: ursulagoff.com (HTTP 503 on two attempts, with and without www); caitlinfordhair.com (only the page title "Caitlin Ford Hair" came through, likely JavaScript-rendered); vividlywickedhair.com and its /new-client/ page (empty body twice); hairbykaseyoh.com (DNS did not resolve; this was a guessed domain and may not exist).
- No brand-affiliated artist list (Pulp Riot, Pravana, Danger Jones, Arctic Fox, Lunar Tides ambassador pages) or awards list (BTC One Shot, NAHA) was successfully used to source sites; searches for these returned product pages rather than artist directories. The sample is therefore weighted toward working local vivid specialists found through search, not toward industry-famous educators.
- Visual quality (photography, type, colour, spacing) was not observed for any site.
- Home pages for Sunday Hair Studio, The Decora House and Justine Mayer were truncated, so their full section counts are unknown.

## What is on their home page versus sub-pages? How many sections is the home page?

### Takeaway
Home pages on the better-organised sites hold only: identity line (who, what specialty, where), one or two images, a short "why me" statement, social proof, and one CTA to the new-client page. Pricing, policies, FAQ, vivid education and the intake form all live one click away. Observed section counts: 3 (HDH), about 5 (Justine Mayer, visible part), 8 to 9 (Nikki, Bold Hearts, CRIS HAIR, Decora House), 10 to 11 (BK), 14 or more (Taylor Monroe, Cherry Blossom).

### Inferences
- Recurring pattern for a consultation-first vivid specialist, synthesised from Nikki, HDH, Sunday, Decora House and Shelley Gregory: (1) home page of roughly 3 to 9 short sections: specialty plus city plus "private studio" line, one strong image or a few, a one-sentence point of difference, reviews, single CTA to new clients; (2) New Clients page: reassurance, who it is and is not for, 2 to 3 numbered steps, first-visit length and cost, deposit, then the form or text instructions; (3) a vivid or colour FAQ page written as client questions covering cost logic, session length, damage, fading and upkeep; (4) a policies page of about 300 to 400 words; (5) about page with a personal label; (6) portfolio as a grid, a few labelled images, or a hand-off to Instagram.
- What the better ones keep off the home page: price lists, deposit and cancellation rules, FAQ accordions, the intake form itself, hair-history questions, long biography and credentials, aftercare instructions, shop/product links, recruitment or booth-rental notices, and public booking-software links (on form-gated sites).
- A portfolio where each look has a name or category (Shelley Gregory, CRIS, BK) reads as more curated than an uncaptioned grid, and suits vivid work where the "look" is the product. This is an inference; image quality was not observed.
- The warmest new-client pages (Nikki, Sunday) remove a decision from the client ("you don't need to know exactly what service to choose- that's my job."), which fits long, custom colour work where clients cannot self-select a service.
- Consistency (one phone number, one booking route, one set of policy terms) is a low-cost marker of polish; every contradiction found was on a site that repeats the same information in multiple places.

### Gaps
- "Polished" versus "cluttered" here is judged on structure, copy and consistency only; no visual assessment was possible, so a structurally tidy site could still look dated, and vice versa.
- The sample lacks the most industry-famous vivid educators' client-facing sites (several did not load or have moved to education/coaching), so the patterns describe working local specialists more than celebrity colorists.
- No analytics or outcome data links any of these structures to enquiry or booking rates.
